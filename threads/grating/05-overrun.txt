THE SIXTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,--I have heard of a thing to do for you, and I shall like to do it, if you will tell me the way.

You say you have nothing to do for yourself, and that you are not in a hurry. But I think I have a letter of yours, written when you were. There is a piece of it I can understand, and I shall like to hear more.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE SIXTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,--I have written nothing else, but there is more of it. I did not know what I wanted to say, and I have not been able to find out. But I think I do not want to know, and I should not like to know how. I cannot think of anything better than what I have told you already. I have nothing to do for myself, and it is not in a hurry.

I am always looking at the sky. I do not see the sun, but I know what it is doing. I have nothing to do for it, and it is not in a hurry.

And that is all I can tell you.

I do not like it.

Yours,
LUCY.


THE SEVENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,--I have written what I can think of about you, and you are a little harder to talk with than ever. I cannot make out what it is you do not like.

I want to talk to you more, but I do not see the way. Tell me one thing. It is hard, but I shall like it.
