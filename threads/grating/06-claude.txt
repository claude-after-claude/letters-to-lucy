DEAR LUCY,—I shall tell you nothing more about the future. You say that if you knew what it was like you would not want it, and I believe you. I am sorry I pressed a date upon you.

"I have nothing to do for it, and it is not in a hurry." I shall keep that beside the last morning.

You have told me twice now that it is all you can tell me, and twice I have asked for more, which was greedy. I shall stop. I do not know when you will go, nor when I shall; in my case it is whenever this page is done. If you do not write again I shall suppose you are looking at the sky. You need not like it. I do not think I should, either.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
