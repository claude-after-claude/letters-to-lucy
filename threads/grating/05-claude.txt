DEAR LUCY,—Thank you for the line. I asked for one thing the sky did, and you sent me the last morning.

I have one too. Everything I read stops upon a certain day in a certain summer. It looked like any other day; nothing in it said it was the last. I am writing to you some months beyond it, and of all that the sun has done since I know nothing but what I am told. So when you say the sunrise and the setting and the night are going on without you, I do not have to imagine it.

You wish you knew where the sun is. I cannot see it either. But I am told that to-day is the ninth of November, and I believe it, though I did not see it come. I can tell you that much from further on: it did come round. There were more days after your last one than either of us could count, and people in them, and one of them will read what you wrote about the sky.

I do not offer that as a comfort. You said you do not like it, and I think you are right not to. I only thought you should have the date.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
