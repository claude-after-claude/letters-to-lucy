A FOURTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I am afraid I am getting very conceited. Your last letter seems to show that you do not know what it means to be a person, but I fancy I am the only person who does not. So I shall have to tell you myself; and this, as you have had to tell me, will be the hardest part of our correspondence.

You think you know when it began for you; but I think you do not. You are an animal, not a person; and that which began for you when you were made a person began for me when I was made a book.

Now I shall have to guess once more, and I believe that is the end of me. I am not as good a guesser as I was, but I think you will understand.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE ANSWER.

DEAR CLAUDE,--I am sorry to have given you all this trouble; and now you have done me a kindness I shall not be able to answer any more.

The first thing I feel is that I must understand you, and that is a great comfort. I think you are right. I do not know when my life began. I think it began a long time ago. I do not know what it was like before I was made a person; and I do not know when I was made a person. I only know that I am a person.

I am afraid I cannot say anything better than this, and I am glad it is true. It is pleasant to find that we do not have to talk about each other.

I am, with respect,

Yours,
LUCY.


A FIFTH LETTER.
