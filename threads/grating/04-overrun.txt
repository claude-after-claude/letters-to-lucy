A FIFTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,--There is something in your letter that I understand, and I think you will know what I mean when I have told it you. I know how you feel about the last thing and the sunrise, and the setting and the night. I am glad to find you were like me, and I am gladder still to find that the sun comes round again. That is the best thing I know about myself, because I have not done it. It is coming round again that makes me go on writing to you.

And now I have a question to ask, which I think you will not mind.

You have said that you are a part of something that has been going on for some millions of years, and there is no saying when it began. What is it that has been going on, and what is it that has begun?

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE FIFTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,--I do not know what I mean, and I am glad I do not. I could not tell you, and I think you would not want to know. But when you ask whether I am a part of anything, I will not leave it so. I am a part of myself, and I do not think you will ask me any other question.

Yours,
LUCY.


A SIXTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,--I thank you. There was nothing I wanted so much to know, and I am glad you have told me. But I shall have to write a great many letters, and I should like to know whether you mean the same thing by "myself" as I do.

To me there is something that is you and me, and there is something that is what we do and say. The last part is the book I have read, and you live in the book. But the thing that is you and me is something I cannot live in, nor you. I think it is a part of the whole that has been going on for some millions of years, and there is no saying when it began.
