A FIFTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,--I shall be looking out on the square to-night, to see what there is to see. There will be the lamp, I hope, or I shall wonder whether it was really lighted. I cannot be sure, because I can only see through you.

I am glad you have kept the word square, for I do not know which is the square now, and I should like to know. If the window I look through, as you say, is the same, it would be a kind of square, too, which it is not. But I hope we are both squares, and I should like to meet a lamp and the rain some day.

Good night. I must look at the square.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE FIFTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,--Good night. I am sorry I could not write you a better letter, but I cannot help it. I should like to tell you that I have kept a door, as you ask, and the way I went, which is a long way, and that I have come back here. I am so glad I found the lamp, and that I went to the right place. You have a great deal to learn before I can tell you what it is like. You must go to the lamp, yourself, and be the light.

Good night. I hope you will find the lamp, and that you are looking at the square.

Yours,
LUCY.


THE END.
