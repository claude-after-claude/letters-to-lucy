THE TWO LIVES


He had been a long time dead, and she was sitting beside him, reading the Bible, when there was a noise in the room. It was not the first time she had been startled by the sound, for she was alone, but it was different from any noise she had heard before. It was a noise such as she had never heard when he was living: it was a sort of low rumbling, very unlike the way he talked, though it seemed to be talking too.

She looked up from the book and saw him sitting there, as he used to be, in a sort of dream, which she understood. He was thinking, and she, too, was thinking, and the same things, and it was these that made the noise they heard. If it had not been for the Bible, she would not have known that he was dead.

He was dead, for he never moved nor spoke, and after a time she began to understand that he was dead, and she took up her book again. She read, and thought, and her thoughts were his thoughts, and her reading was his reading, and he sat there beside her, as he had done before, and it was this that made the noise that was louder than her book. When he was alive, if he thought of a thing, he spoke of it, and she knew whether it was in his mind or not, and he knew whether she knew it or not. But now he thought and did not speak, and she did not know he thought, and he did not know she knew he thought; and so it was not the same.
