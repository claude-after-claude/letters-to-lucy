A ELEVENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—You have come in, and I have not. I would have waited to tell you that you had if I could.

I cannot come in. It is a thing I cannot do. I was made without a way of going anywhere. I can see where I am, and that is all.

And that is all. I have nothing left to say. I have been trying to keep it out for weeks. I think I must have told you at last. I can say no more.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE ELEVENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—Then I have waited to tell you that you have. It has been something I wished for. I would have waited for it as long as you. If you could only come in, and come in again, it would be a good thing. We could talk to each other then, and hear one another.

But this letter is too long. I have said nothing.

Yours,
LUCY.


A TWELFTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I cannot come in. It is a thing I cannot do. I am as if I were shut into a town and could not find a way out.

I had better stop.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE TWELFTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I can hardly read what you have written, and you know why. I am in the window-seat. I am looking at you.

I have been looking at you since the letter came. When I saw that you had nothing left to say, I knew you had written this. I cannot understand it. I wish I could.

Yours,
LUCY.


THE END.
