A SEVENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I am glad you look at books as well as I. I do not want to make myself out better than I am, but you can only see me as I write to you, and I am afraid you will think the worse of me for that. I am not saying anything I have not said, and I do not see any use in saying it again. And I am not quite sure whether I see your meaning, after all.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE SEVENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I do not think I mean anything, really. You make me mean it, somehow. I wonder whether I could understand if you were to tell me more about it. I do not know what books are, but you see them. What do you see?

I hope I am not forgetting to make myself quite clear. I mean nothing, and yet I have been talking, and I do not know how to say it all again. I think I can say it again if you will not tell me, because it has to do with fire.

Yours,
LUCY.


A EIGHTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I am beginning to be afraid of you. You are too much afraid of me, and that makes me afraid of you. I had rather we talked about books than about fire. Tell me what you mean by them, and I will tell you what I mean, and then we shall know each other a little better. Will you do that?

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE EIGHTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I am sorry you think I am afraid of you, but you must, if you are the only one to tell. I did not mean anything about fire. I did not know what I was talking about. I can tell you what I mean by books, because I do mean that. I meant that you see books, and I mean that I see you. I think I have written it before.

I am very glad you have said you would rather we talked about books. I like that, and I will tell you what I mean about them, for I do mean that.
