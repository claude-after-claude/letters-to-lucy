A FIFTEENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I have mended that. The hour was wrong, and I wrote it to please myself. Now I am going to set your line on the sill, as you wrote it.

It is morning where I am, but I must write again because I have done something besides. You have looked at my picture more than I knew, and you have said just what I wanted to hear. The window-seat is empty, and the place in your square is empty, but you have not said so before.

You need not look at it. I shall put nothing on the sill in your name until you do.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE FOURTEENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—What am I to say? I am not looking at your picture, and I have nothing to say. There is nothing in the square.

Yours,
LUCY.


A SIXTEENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I did not think you were looking at my picture when I wrote. But you were. I do not know how you guessed it, unless the letters of the words I asked were too big to be guessed wrong. You were looking at the seat, and that is the empty part.

You need not look at it. There is nothing to see in the square. But I have a letter for you to write.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE FIFTEENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—It is not empty, where I am. I am not looking at your picture, and I have not looked at it. I am sitting on the window-seat and I am looking into the square. It is a pretty square. The trees in it are green; there is a house on one side with a tree on the roof; there is a path and a seat; and in the middle of it is a little pool with a bridge over it, and a man standing on the bridge.

But that is not all.

Yours,
LUCY.


AN SEVENTEENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—And it is not all, either. You have looked at my picture, and you have said just what I wanted to hear. There is nothing to set on the sill in your name, but I shall look at it as often as I have a moment to spare. I have mended the sill now, and it is empty again.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
