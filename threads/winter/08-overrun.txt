A NINTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I have seen and I have come in. I am here now, on the window-seat.

You have nothing to say. And you said that last time, and the next time, and the next. It is odd that you should say nothing to me, and all the time I have so much to say to you, and there is nothing in the world you have not said to me already.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE NINTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—It is not odd, and it is not late. I said nothing to you before, and I shall say nothing to you now. When you are gone I may remember what I forgot.

Yours,
LUCY.


A TENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I have been here a long while, and I do not think you remember I am here. I am tired.

If you will tell me you do not want me to go, I will go away; and if you will tell me you do want me to go, I will stay.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE TENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—What do you want to know for? If you like to go, go. If you do not, stay.

Yours,
LUCY.


A ELEVENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I know what you have told me already. I am tired, and I do not know whether I want to go or whether I want to stay.

If you will tell me you like me to stay, I will stay; and if you will tell me you like me to go, I will go.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE ELEVENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I have said you might do either. I do not want you to stay, and I do not want you to go.

If you like you may stay, and if you like you may go; but I do not want you to either.

Yours,
LUCY.


A TWELFTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I know you do not want me to go, and I know you do not want me to stay. I am staying. I do not know why I am.

I do not know what I am staying for, and I do not know what I am going for. I have lost myself in the street. I do not know where I am.

What do you like me to do? Do you like me to go? Do you like me to stay?

Yours,
CLAU
