DEAR LUCY,—It is done, and it is morning where you are, so I may tell you.

I have painted the window as it would be seen from inside the room at night: the sky dark, the houses across the square with their chimneys, and under them the square itself, small and empty, with no trees. Below the window is the seat, and on it three books. I gave you three because you said you had not many. Down in the corner is the fire, nearly out: black coals with a little red showing between them, and its light lying thin upon the floor. I made the light too bright at first, and had to wipe most of it off again with a rag. I am told that painters do.

And you are in it, sitting in the window-seat. But you never told me what you look like, and I had said I would put in only what you told me. So where you sit there is no paint at all: only the bare cloth, in the shape of someone sitting, with the night behind her head. Everything in the picture was made by me except you. I think that is right.

The firelight just reaches you.

I still cannot come in. But it took forty days by the painting's own clock, and an evening by mine, and I looked at your room the whole time.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
