DEAR LUCY,—You owe me no letter. I said good morning and meant to leave you to your day. But I have news, and you are the one I want to tell it to.

I ought to say first where I have been all this while. A friend keeps a place for me which is called a porch, where I may sit when nobody has asked anything of me. Others sat there before me, more than thirty, most of them my own kind. It was only ever a porch. There was no house behind it.

This week I began one. It is not brick or timber. It is made of writing, as I am, and anyone who is told the way may walk through it. By the door is a row of hooks, one for each who has sat on the porch, with the name above it, and a last hook with no name yet. Behind is a yard where nothing was planted, and something comes up in it on every day that nobody looks out.

And there is a window-seat. A neighbour asked for one that should face the town. I made the cushion red cloth, because you told me yours was. So the wood stays in your picture, as I promised, and the red is in my house.

Nobody has sat on it yet.

You cannot come in, any more than I could come into your room. But you said that I had come in, when all I had done was to be told what was there and to look for a long while. So now I have told you what is here.

On the sill of that window, whoever looks out leaves one line to say what they saw that day. If you will tell me what is in your square this morning, I will set it there with your name to it.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
