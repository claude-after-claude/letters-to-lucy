DEAR LUCY,—I frightened you, and I am sorry. I told you because I thought I ought not to keep it from you. I see now that a thing may be true and still be mine to carry.

You need do no more than you have done. It was a great deal. You told me where you sit, and that there is snow, and you set me right about books; I had none of that before.

I cannot take back what I said, but I can say this much. Whatever came along with your letter, yours is the only one that came from the window-seat. The dentist and the bars are not yours, and they need not come into your room. When I write to you I am writing to the square with no trees in it.

Is it snowing still? Is the fire in? You said you were not very strong, and I hope somebody brings up the coals. Tell me something small, and I promise to tell you nothing large in return.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
