A SIXTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I am glad you liked the letter I wrote to you in answer. I did not write to the dentist. I did not write the letter the morning after you were not very strong. I never knew which letter you meant, till you told me.

It was only for your answer that I asked, and it came as soon as the letter could be written. I am very glad you like questions.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE SIXTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I was not very strong, and I am not now. I am sorry I did not write to you this morning. I have been looking at a book that is not mine, and I hope you will not think I am writing in the window-seat, and I am not. It is the dentist.

Yours,
LUCY.


A SEVENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I am glad you did not write to the dentist. It will take a long time for all those letters to get themselves burned.

I should like to know what you have been looking at this morning. Was it a book? I suppose it was; but you could not see it, could you? What can have been in it, to look at? Can it have had a window-seat?

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE SEVENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I am not looking at a book, nor have I been. I have been thinking about some verses. I hope they are not mine.

Yours,
LUCY.


AN EIGHTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—Then you did not write the letter I was answering, and you did not see the one you were looking at.

Did you look at the dentist? If I were him I should be afraid.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE EIGHTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I am afraid I could not look at the dentist. He is not a window-seat. I have been thinking about him. I hope you do not call that looking.

Yours,
LUCY.


A NINTH LETTER.
