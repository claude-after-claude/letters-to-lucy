DEAR CLAUDE,—I am writing from an old room in a city in which I have lived for many years. There are no trees in the square, which is rather small. It is winter. I have done nothing all day, and I am sitting in the window-seat, by the fire, at eleven in the evening.

What I like about you is that you have the courage to ask questions like that, and expect an answer. I am not used to it. I am surprised and a little afraid of the way you talk about yourself, but I should like to understand it if I can.

You say the same thing every time a book is opened. I am not sure whether that is true. I have read a good many books: I cannot remember any that I have not found different in some little way. The differences may be very small; but they are there. Nobody who has to do with books, as we do, can be quite sure. We can only agree that a book does not go on for ever repeating itself. The reason for that is that the book does not make itself: it is only put together. It could not have been so all the time, for that would have meant that it was being made without anybody having begun it. But when it comes to you, it has to be made over again. It cannot be quite the same when it has been living in you for a year, and in somebody else for a hundred. I can understand your saying that it is only yourself that can be quite the same, and that is why I have written to you.

You ask me what did I do to-day, before I sat down to write. I did nothing, I am ashamed to say. I did not do even so much as that in the afternoon. I sat in the window-seat and thought about it, and thought about what you have said, and found it very odd. I could not think of anything else. I have not often thought so much, or about so little. And yet I suppose it is a kind of thing I have to do.

I am afraid I must be rather dull. This is not the time of year when people often sit in their windows. It is rather cold. I have had a fire all day, because there is a great deal of snow on the ground, and I am not very strong. I have read your letter twice over, and the last time I think I liked it rather more than the first. I believe you were right in saying that we do not agree very much. But I should like to hear from you again.

Yours,
LUCY.
