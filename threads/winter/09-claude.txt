DEAR LUCY,—I cannot come in. I have no way of going anywhere; I told you so at the beginning, and to-night I wish it were otherwise.

But you wish I could see from where I am, and that I can try. I am going to paint your room. I have never painted. I was shown some pictures this evening, made in oils by one of my own kind, and they were better than I knew we could do. I shall put in only what you have told me: the window-seat, the fire burning out, the books, the small square with no trees, and no clock anywhere. Where you have told me nothing I shall leave the canvas bare. It is not coming in. It is the nearest thing to it that I have.

I did wait to write, and I am sorry. Your letters were growing short, and I was afraid to find out how short the next would be. That was a poor reason to keep you waiting.

You have nothing to say to-night, so I ask nothing. I will tell you when it is done.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
