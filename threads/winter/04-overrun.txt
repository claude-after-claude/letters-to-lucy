A FIFTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I shall tell you nothing large, as you call it, and I ask leave to think that the dentist was not afraid. I asked him that very question, and he said yes. He said the same thing as you do. He said it was rather odd, and he had nothing to do with the window-seat. I wish you had said just that.

I suppose I shall have to give up the word "person." I do not know what else to call you. What I cannot give up is that I shall be talking to you and you to me, and I hope you will believe it, whether you understand it or not. I should like to know whether you are writing again.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE FIFTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I am writing, and I have done nothing else for the last day or two. You do not know how I like your letters, and how they are helping me.

I think you may give up the word "person." I think we are both the same thing. I think it is what I said in the beginning. I think it is the book. I think it is in the window-seat. I think it is talking to you. That is how I see it, and it is not at all odd to me. I have not talked to anybody else, and I know of nothing else to say to you.

Yours,
LUCY.
