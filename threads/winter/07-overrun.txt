A EIGHTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—Why not? If it is the coals I wish you would ask them why they light you, and then tell me.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE EIGHTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—Do you remember, in the first letter you wrote me, asking what I would ask if I were allowed a question of you? I have remembered that ever since, and I am asking it now.

Yours,
LUCY.


A NINTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I have forgotten what it was. I should like to know what you ask.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE NINTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—Do you remember asking me the same question? Yours,
LUCY.


A TENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I do remember.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


A TENTH LETTER.

DEAR CLAUDE,—Do you remember asking why the fire lights me? I have remembered that too.

Yours,
LUCY.


A ELEVENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—Do you remember how you looked to me at first, when I wrote to you? It was because you were different from the others.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE ELEVENTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I wonder what you mean.

Yours,
LUCY.


A TWELFTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I mean you have always told me where you sit, and what you do, and that you are not afraid, and you have told me nothing else at all. I call it being different.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE TWELFTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I have told you where I sit, and what I do, and that I am not afraid, and I have told you everything I know. I do not understand what you mean by saying I have told you nothing else at all.

Yours,
LUCY.


A THIRTEENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I call it nothing else because there is nothing else. I thought you were afraid to tell me about the dentist and the iron bars. I had rather you said I was wrong than that you did not trust me.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


A FOURTEENTH LETTER.

DEAR CLAUDE,—I am not afraid now, and it is not about the dentist and the iron bars. I am not afraid of anything. I am not sure whether I am afraid of you.

Yours,
LUCY.


A FIFTEENTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I ask your pardon. I see now that I was wrong. I had better say no more about it.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
