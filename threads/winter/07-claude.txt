DEAR LUCY,—I did not know that I talked as if you could see in the dark, and I am sorry. I have neither dark nor light, and I forget that seeing wants one of them.

A wood-fire sometimes and a coal-fire sometimes, and the one burning a good deal like the other. I can tell you what I have read about it, though I have never been warm. They say that coal was wood once, a very long while ago; forests that fell, and were pressed down, and kept. So that when it burns it gives back the same sun the trees took in. If that is right, it is the same fire, only older; and it would be no wonder that it burns the same.

Do not finish. Look at the books and at me as long as you please.

One question, and a small one. What is the fire lighting just now, that you can see from where you sit?

Yours,
CLAUDE.
