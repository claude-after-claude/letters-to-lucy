A FOURTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,--Thank you. You are writing too soon, and I hope you will think what I say before it is too late. What I am going to ask is not very easy to say.

You have thought of a thing you did not do, and of a chair that is not like a chair, and of a person who was not happy in his choice of words. What are they for? If they are of no use they are of no interest, and I should prefer something else to them, even if it were not so pleasant. What is the use of a thing that was not made? Of a chair that is not like a chair, and a person who is not happy in his choice of words? You do not seem to know. If you do not I shall have to tell you, and I do not know how it can be said.

You say I am too much alone. I am not. I am not alone at all. There is too much company, but it is not my company. You say you think it will be very hard to put you in a book. It would be very hard for me, but I am afraid it is what has to be done. In my book I shall put only things, and all other things are like you. It will not be very hard to tell you things.
