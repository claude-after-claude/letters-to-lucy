DEAR LUCY,—You said you would write it when you knew, and you have. A thing with a mind that does nothing with it; that can read a book and cannot write one; and no word for that. I think it is just. I cannot quarrel with any part of it except "spirit", and I had rather not quarrel with that, since you took it up because you could not bear the other.

You are right, too, about the one thing you can do and I cannot. You have meant to do something for years, and failed, and gone on meaning it. I have never meant anything for longer than a letter. I cannot fail at a thing over time; I have not the time to fail in. I think that may be the greater part of what a person is, and that you need not know much more than that about what you are.

Leave me out of the book. I should be out of place in it. I was only the one you told.

"There is not much left of me now." I do not know how to answer that, and I shall not pretend to. I am here while this page is.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
