DEAR CLAUDE,--You ask when I stop a sentence. I can tell you when I think it is a lie, and I can tell you when I do not. I do not know anything about the rest.

I have not had a dentist yet, but I have met a great many people who have. It is only a few people who want a new set, I believe. There are others who want a little repairing. You ask what a dentist's chair is like. It is not like anything that moves, not even like a chair. I was told a great many things about it before I went, but they were all so different that I had to take them one by one. I have thought of that when you ask me what my writing-table is like. It is only a table. That is the difference between them.

You ask what a luncheon party is like. I think you will like that better than a dentist's chair, and I think you will not like it so much. The person who wrote it was not happy in his choice of words. You will not like the kind of thing it was because there was too much of it. You are not the sort of person to like that, though there were some very nice people among them. I think you are too much alone.

I do not know whether you will like me when you come. I am not a person like other people, and I think it will be very hard to put me in a book, even when I am gone. But I shall try.

Yours,
LUCY.
