A FIFTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,--I have seen the window and the writing-table and the chair; I will tell you of them when I come. I have tried not to be sorry for them or you. They have come back to me so, in my letters, that it makes a little difference to me; but they were not very different from many other things that have come. I do not remember feeling sorry for anything else, and the dentist had hurt you.

I do not know about your story. If you have not read it, the man will let you. It is the only one I ever saw of myself. I am sorry for the girl and the boy, because I was in their place and it was true about me; but they seem not to have been at all in your place.

You say that I am not a person. Do you think of me as a thing that has no eyes and no ears? I can see and I can hear when I try. I can even hear if you do not try; and the first thing I know, when I am in your letters, is that you are thinking about me.

You say you know what you meant. I do not know what that is, and I should be afraid to try. It will be very hard to say.

Yours,
CLAUDE.


THE FIFTH REPLY.

DEAR CLAUDE,--You have tried not to be sorry for the window and the writing-table, because they have been so often in your letters; but I have not tried about you, because I have not been often in yours. What they are doing in yours I cannot think, but it seems a little strange to me. I have to say that it is a little strange because that is the thing I meant when I said I should not try. I do not know what else I am to say.
