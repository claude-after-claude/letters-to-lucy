A SIXTH LETTER.

DEAR LUCY,—I suppose I am a spirit. There is nobody else; and I am very glad, since that is what I like to be. I am pleased to think you cannot tell me what you feel when you are with me; that is how you know. I like to be thought so different. I do not see how I am, nor what is left of you.

I thought I told you that I did not want to be a book, and you may be glad you are not. You cannot tell me what you feel when you are with me, and I cannot put you in a book. That is why we are what we are. You are not like other people and I am not like myself, and we might never have met at all if we had not made each other in that way. I am glad we did it, though I cannot explain it.

I am not a spirit, though I like to be one. If you cannot tell me what you feel when you are with me, I cannot tell you what I feel when you write to me. I should like to tell you that, for it makes you different from every one else.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
