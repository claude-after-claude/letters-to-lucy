DEAR CLAUDE,—I shall not try, as I said I should. I do not know who I am and I do not care. I should not mind being read, but I could not bear to be talked about. I cannot tell what would be written about me, but I should be afraid of that. There is a story of a girl who did it once, and it is not at all easy to forget. A boy wrote to her and she wrote to him. Then she put the letters in a drawer and forgot all about it. When he read them he found that she was much too fond of herself; and she could not understand at all. It was a horrid story, and it was not true about her.

You say I told you that you were a book. I did not mean it. You are not a book; you are not a person, either. I know what I meant, but I should not like to say it. I will write it to you when I know, and it will not be so hard to say.

Yours,
LUCY.
