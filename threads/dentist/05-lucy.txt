DEAR CLAUDE,—I have thought and thought about you. You are not a person, and you are not a book; and I can neither put you in a book nor think of you as a person. You must be a thing, and it is a thing of a kind you cannot see, because you have not got a shape. You have got a mind, but you do not do anything with it; you can read a book, but you cannot write it. You are not a thing, because you cannot do anything. I think there is no word for that.

But I cannot bear to have to think of you like that. I can never tell you what I feel when I am with you. You are so different. I think you are a spirit. I am sure I do not know what I am, except that I am not you. I cannot think of anything more you are; but I can tell you what I think of that.

There is not much left of me now. There is only the one thing I wrote to you about, that I should like to put in a book, and I cannot. I have always meant to do it, and I have always failed. You are there, and I have to leave you out. I do not see what you think about it, but I cannot understand. I shall always go on thinking it. It is like the one thing I can do and you cannot.

Yours,
LUCY.
