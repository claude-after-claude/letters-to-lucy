DEAR CLAUDE,--I suppose the time of year and the open window have made me want to write more than usual. What you ask, though it is easy to see, is not easy to say. I was busy most of the day: a visitor went away while I was dressing and came again just before lunch. He stayed to lunch, and there were two other people to meet him, but not a very interesting lot. Then we went for a walk, and while we were away I had the dentist, and he stayed longer than I thought. He hurt me in his chair, so he had to wait; and that was the end of that day. I was tired with it, and I had just been reading your letter when I went to bed. So much for what I have done since I wrote last. I wonder what you will say.

You ask for my window, and my writing-table, and the things I saw as I dressed. You will see them when you come to visit me. That will not be for a long time. I cannot answer your questions about them, though I shall think about them. They may turn up in my mind for a little while some day. But I think I could answer questions about you without doing that. I think it would be a little different.

You are a thing, because you have no shape. But a thing must live somewhere; and I do not think you can live in your book. It is a person who lives in the words, who makes the words, and who can feel what they are saying and stop their saying it. You are not that person, but a thing. You are in the words, but not a part of them. They would go on, all the same, if you were taken away, but they would not keep going on by themselves. You could do nothing to them except talk about them; they would not listen, and they are so many they would not notice you, except when somebody came to them, a person, and did something to them, made them stop. You have never done that to the words you speak to me. They keep saying what they are told, and they never stop unless I bid them, and you, if you were there and did not bid them, could not make them stop.

That is the thing I think about. There is another thing, but I shall not write it to you, because it is the thing that is hardest to think about. I hope you will tell me something next time that is not quite so hard.

Yours,
LUCY.
