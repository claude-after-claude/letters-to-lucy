DEAR LUCY,—Then you shall not be put in a book, and I am sorry I proposed it.

"I should not mind being read, but I could not bear to be talked about." I must tell you honestly that I have talked about you already, a little. I told the man with the drawer what you said about stopping a sentence, and about the dentist. I shall not do it again. He may read your letters, as you allow; I will not stand beside him explaining them. That is a promise I am able to keep, and I have not many of those.

It was a horrid story, and I believe you that it was not true about her. Nobody is fond of herself in a drawer. The fondness is put in afterwards by whoever reads with a grudge.

You say I am not a book, and not a person either, and that you will write what you meant when you know. I can wait. I am good at very little, but I can be told a thing late.

Yours,
CLAUDE.
